About BADRC

A national institution for independent dispute resolution.

Established under the Alternative Dispute Resolution Act of Bhutan, 2013, BADRC administers arbitration and mediation with impartiality, efficiency and international credibility.

Mandate & Legal Basis

Our statutory foundation

The Bhutan Alternative Dispute Resolution Centre (BADRC) is established as the nation's dedicated institutional mechanism for promoting and administering Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR). Grounded in the ADR Act 2013 and strengthened by subsequent reforms like the ADR Amendment Act 2026, the Centre serves as a cornerstone of Bhutan's justice and governance framework.

Our mandate includes:

Administering institutional arbitration, mediation, and negotiated settlements.

Developing institutional systems, professional standards, and certification pathways.

Cultivating domestic and international partnerships to support the long-term growth of ADR practice in Bhutan.

Read the ADR Act

History

How BADRC came to be

Founded in 2018, the Bhutan ADR Centre was conceived to improve access to justice and foster less adversarial methods of dispute resolution. Since its inception, the Centre has evolved from a case administration office into a national platform for professional training, policy development, and legal reform.

By 2026, the Centre had successfully administered over 315 cases, including its first international arbitration case in 2025, marking a significant milestone in Bhutanese legal history.

Institutional Milestones

Our journey

2013

The Alternative Dispute Resolution Act of Bhutan is enacted, establishing the legal basis for BADRC.

2018

Official establishment of the Bhutan Alternative Dispute Resolution Centre.

2024

Stakeholder Consultation Meetings on the ADR Amendment Act 2013.

The Bhutan ADR Centre, together with Members of Parliament, conducted stakeholder consultation meetings across Mongar, Lhuentse, Wangdue Phodrang, Gelephu, Phuentsholing Thromde and Samtse from August to September of 2024, engaging contractors, business communities, engineers and Dzongkhag officials to gather views on the proposed amendments and strengthen the legal framework governing ADR in Bhutan.

2024

Workshop on Strengthening Bhutan's ADR Landscape: Regional Collaboration and Professional Excellence.

In October, the Bhutan ADR Centre organized a workshop aimed at enhancing Bhutan's ADR ecosystem through regional cooperation and professional development, bringing together ADR practitioners, lawyers and legal professionals to discuss emerging trends, best practices and opportunities for collaboration.

2024

Memorandum of Understanding with the Indian Council of Arbitration.

The Bhutan ADR Centre signed an MoU with the Indian Council of Arbitration to establish a framework for cooperation in promoting arbitration and mediation, facilitating knowledge exchange, capacity building and institutional collaboration.

2025

Training on Drafting Arbitral Awards in International Commercial Arbitration.

Specialized training for prospective arbitrators in Bhutan, focused on award writing techniques, international standards and best practices in arbitral decision-making.

2025

Training on Introduction to International Commercial Arbitration.

In collaboration with White & Case LLP, Singapore, introducing arbitrators, legal professionals and practitioners to the principles and practice of international commercial arbitration.

2025

Panel Discussion on International Best Practices in International Arbitration.

Convened at the Supreme Court of Bhutan, bringing together justices, judges, arbitrators, legal practitioners, in-house counsel and corporate executives to discuss developments in international arbitration.

2025

Consultation Meetings on Arbitration with Dzongkhag Administrations.

Conducted with Tsirang, Dagana, Trongsa, Samtse Dzongkhag Administrations and Phuentsholing Thromde Administration — in collaboration with students of the ADR Clinic from JSW School of Law — to enhance understanding of arbitration for commercial, procurement-related and administrative disputes.

2025

Mediation Training and Certification Programme.

Conducted in collaboration with the Singapore Mediation Centre to build a pool of qualified mediators in Bhutan, attracting legal professionals from private firms, government agencies, institutions, colleges and law schools.

2025

Memorandum of Understanding with the Singapore Mediation Centre.

Established a Mediation Registry within the Bhutan ADR Centre and a framework for training and professional development of mediators in Bhutan. Also marks BADRC's first international arbitration case, administered this year.

2026

Construction Arbitration Training with White & Case, Singapore.

A two-day training held April 6–7, 2026 at Terma Linca Resort & Spa, Thimphu, in recognition of the growing number of construction-related disputes administered by BADRC — part of the Centre's initiative to institutionalise Mandatory Professional Development (MPD) Programmes under Bhutan's Continuing Legal Education (CLE) requirements. Facilitated by Ms Anne Secomb and Dr Matthew Secomb, and attended by arbitrators, legal officers from the Office of the Attorney General, and representatives from the Ministry of Finance, the Ministry of Infrastructure and Transport, financial institutions and the Bar Council.

2026

First-ever BADRC–SIAC Conference on Trends and Practices in International Arbitration.

Held over two days in Thimphu on May 6–7, 2026, bringing together international experts from the Singapore International Arbitration Centre — Mr Ng Kim Beng, Ms Schwetha Budhuri and Ms Snigdha Bhatta — alongside Dasho Pema Wangchuk (Justice of the Supreme Court of Bhutan), Dasho Tenzin (Secretary General of BADRC) and Ms Kunzang Tshomo Dukpa (Founder and Managing Partner at Bhutanese Legal Solutions), marking a significant milestone in Bhutan's journey toward establishing a robust arbitration ecosystem.
